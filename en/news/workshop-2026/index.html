Workshop Report: Quantum Technology × Music

Exploring connections between quantum technology, music, and creative expression through interdisciplinary dialogue and live demonstrations

Date
Saturday, October 10, 2026 · 14:00–18:00
Venue
Room V105, Information Value Creation Education Building, Kobe University
Format
In-person international interactive workshop

Overview

On October 10, 2026, Kobe University hosted an international interactive workshop on quantum technology, music, and creative expression. The event brought together researchers and students from scientific and technological fields, people involved in music and the arts, and members of the wider public to explore emerging connections between quantum computing and creative practice.

Through introductory and invited talks, demonstrations, poster presentations, and musical performances, the workshop offered opportunities not only to present research but also to exchange ideas across disciplinary boundaries.

Talks, posters, and interdisciplinary exchange

The opening presentations introduced fundamental ideas in quantum computing and their applications to music creation, establishing common ground for participants with different backgrounds. Invited talks and demonstrations then explored connections between quantum technologies and creative expression from a range of scientific and artistic perspectives.

During the poster session, presenters and participants engaged in approximately one hour of direct discussion around research presentations and demonstrations. The opportunity for researchers in quantum information science and engineering to exchange perspectives with musicians and artists was a distinctive aspect of the workshop.

Interactive music performance using a real quantum computer

As a practical exploration of the relationship between quantum computing and musical expression, Satofumi Souma and Eduardo Reck Miranda presented an interactive live performance involving a remote, real quantum computer.

In the demonstration, Souma's cello performance was captured through an audio interface and processed in real time by a Python program. The resulting performance data were sent via a MIDI interface to Miranda's system, which was connected to a real quantum computer located overseas. Results obtained from the quantum computation during the performance were then incorporated into the sound produced by a synthesizer, enabling a live session between cello and synthesizer.

By integrating acoustic performance, real-time signal processing, remote quantum computation, and electronic sound generation within a single performance setup, the demonstration explored an approach in which quantum computation contributes to music as it is performed, rather than being used solely to generate musical material in advance.

Looking ahead

The workshop created a valuable opportunity to consider connections between quantum technology and music or the arts from the complementary perspectives of research, implementation, composition, and performance. EntangleTune will continue to pursue research and development that supports such interdisciplinary exchange and explores new possibilities for musical and creative expression using quantum technology.
